## Cảnh 01 · Title
Thời lượng: 4
Hình: Thẻ giấy: "Why do you stay up late on your phone?" — ánh sáng xanh nhạt hắt từ dưới lên như màn hình.

## Cảnh 02 · Opening story
Hình: phong-ngu.jpg — cậu bé nằm, tư thế dien-thoai; đồng hồ 23:00 → 23:47 → 00:58 → 01:40; báo thức "7:00"; mắt díp lại.
> Eleven at night. You're in bed, and you are exhausted.
> Your alarm is set for seven. You know you should sleep.
> So you pick up your phone. Just to check one thing.
> One video. Then another. Then a message, then a feed.
> Your eyes are burning. You tell yourself, just one more.
> You look up at the clock. It's one forty in the morning.
> The next day, you drag yourself through class or work.
> And that night? You do it all over again.
> You're not even enjoying it that much.
> So why can't you just put the phone down?

## Cảnh 03 · Not a rare problem
Hình: BẢNG: tên gọi "BEDTIME PROCRASTINATION" viết lên; cụm người; câu trích trong bong bóng: "I want to go to bed on time, but I just don't." [1]
> If this sounds like you, you're far from alone.
> In twenty fourteen, researchers in the Netherlands gave it a name.
> Bedtime procrastination.
> It means going to bed later than you planned, when nothing is actually stopping you.
> No baby crying. No deadline. Just you, and a glowing screen.

## Cảnh 04 · Clue one: not about sleep
Hình: BẢNG: giường bên trái "SLEEP" (không gạch); bên phải danh sách việc đang làm, mũi tên "STOP?" bị kẹt; hai thanh tương quan nhỏ. [1]
> Let's collect some clues.
> Clue one. People who delay their bedtime often agree with a simple sentence.
> I want to go to bed on time, but I just don't.
> In surveys, putting off bedtime was linked to sleeping too little and feeling tired the next day.
> But the researchers noticed something.
> People weren't saying they didn't want to sleep.
> They seemed to struggle with stopping whatever they were doing.

## Cảnh 05 · Clue two: three kinds of late
Hình: BẢNG: ba thẻ: "ON PURPOSE" (cậu bé ôm ly cacao, đồng hồ tự do), "LOST TRACK" (đồng hồ tan chảy mờ), "WAITING TO FEEL SLEEPY" (cừu đếm). [3]
> Clue two. Another team sat down with seventeen people who often went to bed late.
> They asked them why.
> Some stayed up on purpose. Evening was the only time that felt like their own.
> Some simply lost track of time.
> They were absorbed, and suddenly it was two in the morning.
> And a few waited until they felt really sleepy, so they wouldn't lie awake in bed.

## Cảnh 06 · Clue three: the color of the screen
Hình: BẢNG: ba nhóm cột: "NO PHONE", "PHONE + NIGHT SHIFT", "PHONE"; ba cột gần bằng nhau; nhãn "167 YOUNG ADULTS · 7 NIGHTS". [6]
> Clue three. Many phones have a night mode that makes the screen warmer and less blue.
> In twenty twenty-one, a study tested it on one hundred sixty-seven young adults.
> For a week, some used their phone before bed with night mode on.
> Some used it with night mode off. And some didn't use a phone at all.
> Their sleep was tracked with a band on the wrist.
> Across the whole group, sleep looked about the same in all three groups.

## Cảnh 07 · Clue four: the tired brain
Hình: BẢNG: não; vùng thưởng sáng rực "REWARD ▲"; vùng trán mờ dần "BRAKES ▼"; hình ảnh dễ chịu được đánh dấu tick nhiều hơn. [7]
> Clue four. In a brain scanning study, some young adults stayed awake all night.
> The next day, they rated a series of pictures in the scanner.
> In the sleep-deprived group, the brain's reward areas reacted more strongly to pleasant pictures.
> At the same time, those reward areas were less linked to regions at the front of the brain.
> Those front regions help you weigh choices and put on the brakes.
> And the tired group judged more of the pictures as pleasant.

## Cảnh 08 · The question
Hình: BẢNG: ba phương án A/B/C có biểu tượng (bóng đèn xanh / bàn đạp phanh + đồng hồ / viên thuốc trên điện thoại).
> So why do you stay up on your phone, even when you're exhausted?
> A: the blue light from the screen keeps your brain awake.
> B: you're not avoiding sleep, you're avoiding stopping, and a tired brain is bad at stopping.
> Or C: your phone is as addictive as a drug and has hijacked your brain.

## Cảnh 10 · The key
Hình: Cậu bé trong bong bóng suy nghĩ; chữ "GO TO SLEEP" đổi thành "STOP".
> The key is this. Going to sleep isn't something that just happens to you when you're tired.
> It's a decision. A decision to stop.
> And at night, three things are working against that decision.

## Cảnh 11 · Force one: your own time
Hình: BẢNG: thanh ngày 24 giờ chia màu: học/làm, việc nhà, người khác cần; chỉ khúc cuối tối màu "YOURS"; mũi tên kéo khúc đó dài ra qua nửa đêm. [3]
> The first force is time that feels like yours.
> Think about a busy day. Classes, work, chores, people who need something from you.
> For many people, the only free hours come at the very end.
> Going to sleep means ending them.
> So you stretch them, a little at a time.
> Online, people call this revenge bedtime procrastination, taking back the day you didn't get.
> It's the on-purpose group from clue two.

## Cảnh 12 · Force two: no last page
Hình: BẢNG: một cuốn sách có trang cuối "THE END"; bên cạnh cuộn feed dài vô tận chạy mãi xuống dưới, không có đáy; các mũi tên "WHY PEOPLE STOPPED" đều chỉ ra ngoài điện thoại (biểu tượng chung: tin nhắn, lịch hẹn, việc khác). [8][9]
> The second force is that nothing ever ends.
> A book has a last page. A TV show used to end at the hour.
> But a feed has no bottom. And the next video starts before you decide.
> So what makes people stop?
> In one study, researchers followed forty-six people's scrolling for a week.
> Most reasons for stopping came from outside the app.
> Life got in the way. A message, a place to be, something else to do.
> Late at night, in bed, those interruptions rarely come.
> In another study, a reminder popped up after fifteen minutes of scrolling.
> People noticed it, but they often kept scrolling anyway.

## Cảnh 13 · Force three: more gas, less brake
Hình: BẢNG: hai bàn đạp; "GAS" lớn dần, "BRAKE" nhỏ dần theo đồng hồ từ 21:00 tới 01:00. [1][7]
> The third force is the tired brain from clue four.
> Remember, sleep loss turned up the brain's response to reward.
> And it loosened the link to the regions that help you hold back.
> That's like more gas and less brake.
> And here's the timing problem.
> The decision to go to bed comes at the very end of the day.
> It's exactly when you're most tired.
> So the moment you most need your brakes is the moment they're weakest.

## Cảnh 14 · Is willpower a fuel tank?
Hình: BẢNG: bình xăng "WILLPOWER" rơi dần → dấu hỏi; nhãn "23 LABS · 2,141 PEOPLE"; kim gần số 0. [11]
> You might have heard that willpower is like fuel, and by night your tank is empty.
> That idea was popular for years.
> But when twenty-three labs repeated a key experiment with over two thousand people, the effect almost vanished.
> So scientists still debate why self-control feels harder at night.
> Tiredness is a strong suspect. A magic fuel tank, probably not.

## Cảnh 15 · The busy mind
Hình: BẢNG: đầu người nằm trên gối, bên trong là các bong bóng ý nghĩ quay vòng: tập phim, tin nhắn, cảnh hài; nhãn "MIND STILL RUNNING". [4]
> There's one more thing the phone does.
> It keeps your mind busy right up to the moment you close your eyes.
> A survey of young adults found that frequent binge-watching was linked to worse sleep.
> The link ran through a busy mind.
> People's thoughts were still racing when they tried to sleep.
> It wasn't their bodies that stayed wired, it was their heads.

## Cảnh 16 · What about blue light?
Hình: BẢNG: quyển sách giấy vs máy tính bảng; đồng hồ "≈ 10 MIN"; mũi tên melatonin đi xuống; sau đó nhắc lại ba cột Night Shift. [5][6]
> So is blue light innocent? Not quite.
> In one lab study, twelve adults read on a tablet for four hours before bed, five nights in a row.
> Compared with a printed book, they made less of the sleep hormone melatonin.
> Their body clock shifted later, and they took about ten minutes longer to fall asleep.
> That's real. But it's four hours of bright reading, in a lab.
> And remember the night mode study.
> In everyday life, the color of the screen didn't seem to be the main problem.
> Light is one piece. It's just not the biggest one.

## Cảnh 17 · Why it hits young people harder
Hình: BẢNG: biểu đồ đường giờ ngủ theo tuổi; đường đi lên muộn dần từ 10 tuổi, đỉnh khoảng 20, rồi đi xuống; chấm "≈ 20". [12]
> If you're a teenager or in your early twenties, there's another reason.
> A survey of about twenty-five thousand people tracked when people naturally sleep at different ages.
> Through the teenage years, the body clock drifts later and later.
> It peaks around age twenty, then slowly moves earlier again.
> So your body may not feel sleepy at ten, even when you're tired.
> Combine a late body clock with an early alarm, and the evening gets very long.

## Cảnh 18 · Are phones ruining a generation?
Hình: BẢNG: cân hai đĩa: "SCREENS" và "WELL-BEING"; mũi tên nhỏ; nhãn "STILL DEBATED"; sau đó điện thoại đặt trên tủ đầu giường, không sáng, nhãn "IN THE ROOM". [10][15][13]
> Does that mean phones are destroying young people's minds?
> That's still debated among scientists.
> One large analysis of data on over three hundred thousand teens found the average link with well-being was very small.
> Other researchers argue it's bigger for some people.
> Sleep is where the evidence is clearer.
> A review of twenty studies and over a hundred twenty thousand children and teens found a consistent link.
> Using a device at bedtime was tied to roughly double the odds of not sleeping enough.
> Even having the device in the bedroom, without using it, was linked to less sleep.
> These studies show links, not proof of cause. But the pattern keeps showing up.

## Cảnh 19 · The answer
Hình: BẢNG: phương án B sáng lên; A thu nhỏ "A SMALL PIECE"; C có nhãn "TOO SIMPLE".
> So the answer is B.
> You're not really avoiding sleep. You're avoiding the end of your day.
> And you make that choice when your brain is tired, in front of a screen with no last page.
> Blue light plays a small part. And calling it a drug addiction is too simple.

## Cảnh 20 · How to make stopping easier
Hình: BẢNG: bốn thẻ hiện lần lượt: ổ sạc ngoài cửa phòng ngủ; báo thức "BED IN 30 MIN"; đánh dấu "LAST EPISODE"; cốc trà + sách lúc 19:00 "ME TIME EARLIER"; nhãn nhỏ "38 PEOPLE · 4 WEEKS". [14][13][8][3]
> So how do you make stopping easier?
> Don't count on willpower at one in the morning. Make the decision earlier.
> Charge your phone outside your bedroom, so stopping happens by default.
> Set an alarm that says it's time for bed, not just time to wake up.
> Pick your stopping point before you start. This episode, then lights out.
> And try to find some time for yourself earlier in the day.
> In a small trial, thirty-eight people stopped using their phones thirty minutes before bed for four weeks.
> Compared with a group that kept their habits, they reported falling asleep faster and sleeping longer.
> It's a small study. But it's an easy experiment to try on yourself.

## Cảnh 21 · Back to bed
