## Cảnh 01 · Title
Thời lượng: 4
Hình: Thẻ giấy: "Why are you tired after a day of doing nothing?" — cậu bé nằm dài, chữ "Zzz" mờ.

## Cảnh 02 · Opening story
Hình: phong-ngu.jpg — đồng hồ 11:00 → 14:00 → 18:00; cậu bé ngáp, nằm, cầm điện thoại; nhãn "SUNDAY".
> It's Sunday. You wake up at eleven.
> No plans. No homework due. Nobody needs you for anything.
> You move from the bed to the couch.
> A show. Your phone. Another show. A snack.
> You never even leave the house.
> By six in the evening, something strange happens.
> You feel exhausted. Heavy. Like you ran a marathon.
> But you didn't run anything. You barely stood up.

## Cảnh 03 · The Monday puzzle
Hình: BẢNG: hai cột "SUNDAY" (giường, sofa, điện thoại) và "MONDAY" (cặp sách, bóng đá); thanh "ENERGY AT 6 PM": Sunday thấp, Monday cao hơn.
> Now think about Monday.
> School all day. Then football after class.
> You should be wrecked. And yet, that evening, you feel more awake.
> A busy day leaves you buzzing. An empty day leaves you drained.
> That's backwards, isn't it?
> If tiredness meant you used up your energy, resting should fill you up.
> So what's going on?

## Cảnh 04 · Clue one: the brain's energy bill
Hình: BẢNG: hình người, não sáng; thanh "BODY WEIGHT 2%" và "ENERGY 20%"; mũi tên "HARD THINKING: +5% OR LESS (LOCAL)". [1]
> Let's collect some clues.
> Clue one. Your brain is about two percent of your body weight.
> But it uses about twenty percent of your body's energy.
> Here's the surprise. It uses that energy all the time, even at rest.
> When you do a hard mental task, the change is small.
> Blood flow to the busy brain areas often rises by five percent or less.
> Lying on the couch doesn't switch your brain into power saving mode.

## Cảnh 05 · Clue two: the dull task
Hình: BẢNG: người ngồi trước màn hình; đồng hồ đếm "60+ MIN"; nhịp tim chậm dần; nhãn "FEELS LIKE: BOREDOM" thay vì "HARD WORK". [2]
> Clue two. In one study, people did a long, dull monitoring task.
> They watched a screen and responded to targets for over an hour.
> As time went on, their reactions got slower.
> Their heart rate slowed down too. Their bodies were winding down, not working harder.
> And when the researchers asked how it felt?
> It sounded less like hard work, and more like boredom.

## Cảnh 06 · Clue three: the shock button
Hình: BẢNG: phòng trống, ghế, đồng hồ "6–15 MIN"; nút bấm có tia sét; số "12 OF 18 MEN · 6 OF 24 WOMEN". [3]
> Clue three. In twenty fourteen, psychologists asked people to do something very simple.
> Sit alone in a room for six to fifteen minutes. No phone. Just think.
> Most people did not enjoy it.
> In one version, people could press a button to give themselves a mild electric shock.
> Twelve out of eighteen men pressed it at least once.
> So did six out of twenty-four women.
> Some people chose pain over doing nothing at all.

## Cảnh 07 · Clue four: the easy bike ride
Hình: BẢNG: xe đạp tập; nhãn "36 TIRED YOUNG ADULTS · 6 WEEKS"; ba nhóm "EASY", "MODERATE", "NO EXERCISE"; mũi tên mệt giảm mạnh ở "EASY". [5]
> Clue four. Researchers found thirty-six young adults who felt tired all the time.
> They were healthy, but they barely exercised.
> Some rode an exercise bike for twenty minutes, three times a week, for six weeks.
> Some rode at a moderate pace. Some rode at a very easy pace.
> And some didn't exercise at all.
> Both groups who rode felt more energetic.
> The easy riders felt the biggest drop in fatigue.
> And it wasn't because they got fitter. Their fitness change didn't explain it.

## Cảnh 08 · The question
Hình: BẢNG: bốn thẻ manh mối thu nhỏ ở trên; câu hỏi lớn ở giữa; ba thẻ A/B/C.
> So here's the question.
> Why do you feel tired after a day of doing nothing?
> Is it A. Your body still burned through its energy, and your battery ran low?
> B. Tiredness isn't a battery. It's a signal your brain sends, and an empty day sets it off?
> Or C. You slept too much, and extra sleep makes you tired?

Thời lượng: 10

## Cảnh 10 · The key
Hình: BẢNG: biểu tượng "HUNGER" (bụng, đĩa) cạnh "TIREDNESS" (pin có dấu hỏi); chữ "A SIGNAL, NOT A FUEL GAUGE". [6]
> Here's the key idea.
> Think about hunger. You don't feel hungry only when your body runs out of fuel.
> You feel hungry when you smell pizza. Or when it's lunchtime.
> Hunger is a message your brain creates to steer what you do.
> Many researchers think tiredness works in a similar way.
> It's not a fuel gauge. It's a signal.

## Cảnh 11 · The battery myth
Hình: BẢNG: cục pin khổng lồ "BRAIN BATTERY" bị gạch; thanh năng lượng não gần như phẳng giữa "REST" và "THINKING". [1]
> Start with the battery idea.
> If your brain were a phone battery, a lazy day would charge it up.
> But your brain uses roughly the same energy whether you're solving puzzles or staring at a wall.
> So a day on the couch doesn't save much fuel.
> And a busy day doesn't drain much extra.
> The feeling of tiredness must be coming from somewhere else.

## Cảnh 12 · Tiredness as a message
Hình: BẢNG: bảng cân hai đĩa "THIS TASK" và "SOMETHING BETTER?"; đĩa thứ hai nặng hơn → đèn "TIRED" sáng. [6]
> One influential idea goes like this.
> Your brain keeps asking: is this the best use of my time right now?
> When the answer is no, you start to feel effort and fatigue.
> It's your brain nudging you to switch to something else.
> Think of a boring class versus a game you love.
> Same hour, same brain. One feels endless. The other flies by.
> This is still a hypothesis, and scientists debate it.
> But it explains a lot. Tiredness can be about value, not just fuel.

## Cảnh 13 · Boredom is a warning light
Hình: BẢNG: hai đèn cảnh báo "ATTENTION" và "MEANING"; mũi tên "TOO LITTLE" và "TOO MUCH" đều dẫn tới "BORED". [7]
> Now add boredom.
> Psychologists describe boredom as a signal too.
> It appears when your attention can't really grab onto anything.
> That can happen when there's too little going on, or too much.
> It also appears when what you're doing doesn't feel meaningful to you.
> And boredom often feels a lot like being tired.

## Cảnh 14 · Scrolling isn't resting
Hình: BẢNG: điện thoại với dòng tin cuộn; nhãn "LOTS OF INPUT" và "LITTLE MEANING"; dấu bằng "≠ REST". [7]
> Here's the sneaky part.
> A day on your phone doesn't feel empty. It's full of videos and noise.
> But lots of input isn't the same as being engaged.
> Clip after clip, your attention never really settles.
> And very little of it matters to you.
> So your brain lights up both warning lights at once.

## Cảnh 15 · The body winds down
Hình: BẢNG: đường nhịp tim dần thưa; đồng hồ dài; người dần ngả ra ghế; nhãn "UNDER-AROUSED". [2]
> Remember the dull monitoring task?
> When nothing interesting happens, your body seems to power down.
> Your heart slows. You feel heavy and sleepy.
> Not because you worked hard. Because almost nothing asked you to wake up.

## Cảnh 16 · Doing nothing is hard
Hình: BẢNG: bản đồ thế giới với chấm ở 11 nước; nhãn "2,557 PEOPLE · 11 COUNTRIES"; hai cột "THINK" thấp, "DO" cao. [4]
> And remember the people who couldn't sit still?
> The main finding was later tested with over two and a half thousand people in eleven countries.
> In every country, people enjoyed doing something more than just sitting with their thoughts.
> So doing nothing isn't automatically restful.
> For most of us, it's actually kind of unpleasant.

## Cảnh 17 · Movement makes energy
Hình: BẢNG: chồng thẻ nghiên cứu "70 TRIALS · 6,807 PEOPLE" và "81 STUDIES · 7,050 PEOPLE"; mũi tên "ENERGY ▲" "FATIGUE ▼"; chú thích "SMALL TO MODERATE". [9][10]
> Now the bike clue.
> One small study is easy to doubt. But it's not alone.
> In twenty oh six, researchers combined seventy trials with nearly seven thousand people.
> Regular exercise made people feel more energetic and less tired.
> A newer review in twenty twenty-two, with eighty-one studies, found the same pattern.
> The effect is small to moderate. It's not magic.
> But moving seems to tell your brain that the day is worth being awake for.

## Cảnh 18 · Sunday jet lag
Hình: BẢNG: hai thanh giấc ngủ "SCHOOL DAYS 11 PM–7 AM" và "SUNDAY 2 AM–11 AM"; khoảng lệch có nhãn "SOCIAL JETLAG"; máy bay nhỏ. [8]
> There's one more piece. Your body clock.
> On school days, you sleep from eleven to seven.
> On Sunday, you sleep from two to eleven.
> That's like flying to another time zone for the weekend.
> Then on Monday morning, you fly right back.
> Scientists call it social jetlag.
> In surveys, people with more of it tend to sleep worse and feel worse.
> So the problem isn't sleeping too much. It's sleeping at the wrong time for your body.

## Cảnh 19 · Sleep pressure still builds
Hình: BẢNG: đường "SLEEP PRESSURE" đi lên theo giờ thức, giống nhau cho "BUSY DAY" và "LAZY DAY". [11]
> And while you lie around, something else keeps happening.
> From the moment you wake up, pressure to sleep slowly builds in your brain.
> It rises with time awake, whatever you do.
> A lazy day doesn't pause it.

## Cảnh 20 · When it's something else
Hình: BẢNG: ba thẻ xám "SHORT ON SLEEP", "GETTING SICK", "LOW MOOD"; nhãn "NOT EVERY TIRED DAY IS BOREDOM".
> To be fair, not every tired Sunday is about boredom.
> If you slept badly all week, your body may simply need sleep.
> If you're getting sick, tiredness is one of the first signs.
> And feeling flat for a long time can be part of low mood.
> Those are real causes too. Rest and care are the right answer there.
> But if you slept fine, and you're healthy, and you still feel drained after doing nothing?
> That's usually the signal talking.

## Cảnh 21 · The answer
Hình: BẢNG: ba thẻ A/B/C; A và C mờ đi, B phóng to phát sáng; ba biểu tượng nhỏ "BORED", "STILL", "CLOCK OFF".
> So, the answer is B.
> Tiredness isn't a battery reading. It's a signal your brain creates.
> An empty day hits every switch at once.
> Boredom. Hardly any movement. And a body clock knocked off schedule.
> A is wrong, because lying still saves almost no energy.
> And C is only half right. It's not the amount of sleep. It's the timing.

## Cảnh 22 · How to actually rest
Hình: BẢNG: ba thẻ: "SWITCH, DON'T SHUT DOWN" (bút vẽ, sách, bạn bè), "MOVE A LITTLE" (đi bộ 20'), "KEEP YOUR WAKE-UP TIME" (đồng hồ báo thức). [5][7][8]
> So how do you rest without feeling drained?
> First, think of rest as switching, not shutting down.
> Pick something that grabs your attention and means something to you.
> Drawing, cooking, a call with a friend. Not just more scrolling.
> Second, move a little. A walk or an easy bike ride counts.
> In that study, easy was enough.
> Third, try to wake up close to your usual time, even on weekends.

## Cảnh 23 · Back to Sunday
Hình: phong-ngu.jpg — sáng Chủ nhật, cửa sổ nắng; cậu bé dậy sớm hơn, mang giày, cầm bóng; nhãn "SUNDAY, TAKE TWO".
> Next Sunday, you wake up at nine, not eleven.
> You go for a short walk. You spend an hour building something you care about.
> You still watch a show. You still check your phone.
> But at six in the evening, you notice something.
> You're not exhausted. You actually feel awake.
> You didn't need more rest. You needed a better kind.
